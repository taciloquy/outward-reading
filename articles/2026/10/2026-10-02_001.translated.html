How gold surpassed cocaine as Peru's most profitable criminal industry

黄金如何超过可卡因，成为秘鲁最赚钱的犯罪产业

"Halt, stop right there," shouts a Peruvian Navy Seal, firing warning shots into the air.

"站住，就站在那儿，"一名秘鲁海军海豹突击队员喊道，同时朝天鸣枪示警。

But the fleeing man pays no attention, scrambling up to the rim of a football pitch-sized crater and disappearing into the dense rainforest as the commandos give chase, swearing and shooting.

但那个逃跑的人毫不理会，手脚并用地爬上一个足球场大小的矿坑边缘，消失在茂密雨林中，突击队员们一边咒骂一边开枪追击。

Meanwhile, other marines set dynamite charges on a makeshift wooden raft that floats in the crater, which is essentially an enormous pit of opaque, stagnant water.

与此同时，其他海军陆战队员在一个临时木筏上安放炸药——木筏漂浮在那个矿坑里，而矿坑本质上就是一大潭浑浊的死水。

The raft has a diesel-fuelled suction pump motor with thick plastic tubing used to suck up and sift through river sediment for tiny particles of gold.

木筏上有一台柴油驱动的抽吸泵马达，连着粗塑料管，用来抽取并筛分河床沉积物中的微小金粒。

Soon after, a mushroom of orange and black fumes envelops the trees – and seconds later comes the sound of the explosion, shattering the peace of the Tambopata National Reserve, one of Peru’s most prized Amazon parks.

不久之后，一团橙黑混杂的蘑菇状烟尘笼罩了树林——几秒后传来爆炸声，打破了坦博帕塔国家保护区（秘鲁最珍贵的亚马逊公园之一）的宁静。

In total, more than 140,000 hectares have been lost to mining in Madre de Dios – a shocking 97.5% of the total deforestation caused by mining in Peru’s Amazon rainforest, an area twice the size of the US state of California.

在马德雷德迪奥斯，因采矿而失去的土地总计超过 14 万公顷——占到秘鲁亚马逊雨林因采矿造成森林砍伐总量的 97.5%，面积相当于美国加利福尼亚州的两倍。

Military crackdowns on illegal mining in the Amazon are not new in Peru. But the skyrocketing international price of gold has caused this destructive activity to spread, empowering local gangs and attracting transnational organised crime.

在秘鲁，对亚马逊非法采矿的军事打击并非新鲜事。但国际金价飙升让这种破坏性活动不断扩散，壮大了当地帮派，也吸引了跨国犯罪组织。

"Dirty" gold has become the country’s most profitable illicit industry, even surpassing drug trafficking in the world’s second-biggest producer of cocaine after Colombia.

"肮脏的黄金"已成为该国利润最高的非法产业，甚至超过了毒品贩运——秘鲁是仅次于哥伦比亚的世界第二大可卡因生产国。

"Gold prices have risen," said Gen Victor Cotrina, the military commander of Madre de Dios, who is leading the crackdown on illegal gold mining.

"金价涨了，"领导打击非法金矿行动的马德雷德迪奥斯军事指挥官维克托·科特里纳将军说。

"That’s why, if a miner’s motor is destroyed, he’ll replace it quickly because gold is at its peak price: the very next day he’s back to his mining activities.

"所以，如果一个矿工的马达被毁，他会很快换一台，因为黄金正处于价格最高点：第二天他就回去继续采矿了。"

He’s back at work again. "For troops used to combating drug trafficking by targeting well-hidden plantations of coca, which is used to make cocaine, the fight against illegal gold presents new challenges as the end product is not an illicit drug but a legally traded commodity, Cotrina said.

"他又回来干活了。"科特里纳说，对习惯了以打击毒品贩运为目标的部队而言——他们的目标是隐藏得很好的古柯（可卡因原料）种植园——打击非法黄金带来了新挑战，因为最终产品不是违禁毒品，而是一种可以合法交易的商品。

"The difference is that an open-pit mining operation doesn’t require covert operations, whereas drug trafficking does." He said previous anti-mining crackdowns had been ineffective due to corruption and patchy enforcement.

"区别在于，露天采矿作业不需要秘密行动，而毒品贩运需要。"他说，此前打击采矿的行动之所以收效甚微，是因为腐败和执法时断时续。

But now, with "aerial surveillance and military personnel who are available 24/7 to conduct patrols, we are determined to eliminate mining in this region". The evidence is not difficult to find or destroy.

但现在，有了"空中监视和 24 小时待命执行巡逻的军事人员，我们决心消灭这一地区的采矿"。证据既不难找，也不难销毁。

Behind the wall of green that marks the edge of the reserve, troops and environmental prosecutors set ablaze a network of well-supplied mining camps, built with timber and blue tarpaulins and stocked with food and beer.

在标示保护区边界的那道绿色屏障之后，部队和环保检察官点燃了一片供应充足的采矿营地网络——这些营地使用木材和蓝色防水布搭建，储有食物和啤酒。

A lone cockerel crows as the abandoned camps go up in flames.

废弃的营地燃起大火时，一只公鸡在孤立地打鸣。

Two women had been left behind by the fleeing miners: Andrea, 30, wept as she begged the officers not to arrest her, explaining she was a single mother who had been working as a cook and could find no other work to support a sick mother and two children.

逃跑的矿工留下了两名女性：30 岁的安德烈娅哭着求警官不要逮捕她，解释说她是单亲母亲，一直在做厨娘，找不到别的工作来供养生病的母亲和两个孩子。

Liz Compi, 20, was found sitting on a boat with her one-year-old son. She said she had taken the job as a cook for $450 a month after being abandoned by her partner and sacked from a previous job.

20 岁的利兹·孔皮被发现带着一岁大的儿子坐在一艘船上。她说，被伴侣抛弃、又丢了上一份工作后，她接下了这份月薪 450 美元的厨娘工作。

The dirty gold departs Peru as contraband to Bolivia and Brazil, but much of it is laundered into the legal trade via a controversial registration system called Reinfo.

这些"脏金"作为走私品离开秘鲁，运往玻利维亚和巴西；但其中很大一部分通过一个名为 Reinfo 的争议性登记制度被"洗白"进了合法贸易。

The system, in theory, should bring informal miners into the legal economy, but critics say it allows them to continue working without environmental permits and laundering illegal gold through formal companies.

该制度理论上应把非正规矿工纳入合法经济，但批评者说，它让他们得以在没有环境许可的情况下继续作业，并借正规公司把非法黄金洗白。

The trade is so lucrative that members of Congress have backed five repeated extensions of Reinfo, despite pushes to reform or eliminate the registry. Since the beginning of Covid-19 pandemic, the price of gold has tripled.

这门生意利润丰厚，以至于国会议员支持了对 Reinfo 的五次延期，尽管一直有改革或废除该登记的呼声。自新冠疫情开始以来，黄金价格已上涨两倍。

Tens of thousands of miners have poured into protected Amazon parks and Indigenous reserves, stripping away vast tracts of rainforest and leaving behind a denuded, pocked terrain, stagnant pools and disrupted river courses.

数万名矿工涌入受保护的亚马逊公园和原住民保留地，剥去大片雨林，留下满目疮痍、坑坑洼洼的地表、死水潭和被改道的河流。

Mercury is the cheapest and easiest way to extract gold because it binds with the particles in the river sediment.

水银是开采黄金最便宜、最省事的方式，因为它能与河床沉积物中的金粒结合。

"The government’s response is moving very slowly," said Flores after another day of relatively fruitless jungle raids, destroying camps that are likely to spring back up soon after. "Meanwhile, illegal mining operates 24 hours a day.

"政府的反应推进得非常缓慢，"在又一天收效甚微的丛林突袭之后，弗洛雷斯说——他们摧毁的那些营地很可能很快就死灰复燃。"与此同时，非法采矿一天 24 小时不停。"

The miners measure time in grams, not minutes, so we need to rethink how we investigate mining," he said.

"矿工是用克、而不是用分钟来计量时间的，所以我们需要重新思考如何调查采矿，"他说。
