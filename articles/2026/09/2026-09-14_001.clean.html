No one is falling for Trump's budget-busting 'dividend' check gimmick

Socialism will not be stopped with more socialism. President Donald Trump's proposal to pay every adult citizen $5,000 if the GOP holds the House and Senate this fall is a desperate gimmick that betrays a profound lack of seriousness about the looming fiscal crisis. "If the Republicans win, you win with us, and you get $5,000," Trump said Wednesday night at the party's midterm convention in Dallas. He dubbed the payout the "Trump Dividend" and said recipients would have to spend the money inside the United States — but offered no details on how, or whether, Congress would even be asked to authorize it.

This would cost an estimated $1.2 trillion next year, according to the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget — roughly $5,000 times some 245 million adult citizens. The money would need to be borrowed on top of the $40 trillion national debt — and paid back with interest. Even the administration can't agree on where the money is supposed to come from.

Trump said revenue from tariffs would cover what he branded as a "dividend," telling Fox News's Laura Ingraham that "we're taking in $21 trillion" from tariffs. The Treasury's own numbers put net tariff revenue this fiscal year at about $154 billion. Overall tariff and excise collections since the start of last year total just under $500 billion — but more than $100 billion of that has already been refunded after the Supreme Court struck down many of the administration's tariffs, meaning the government refunded more money in some months than it collected.

The taxes on imports that remain in effect are expected to generate less than $200 billion this year, a sum already baked into this year's deficit of more than $2 trillion. The president previously said he'd earmark this same pot of tariff revenue to cover nearly $6 trillion in other costs. It cannot pay for everything twice.

Flooding the economy with money would only worsen inflation, since it would mean more dollars chasing the same amount of goods. That would raise the prices of basic staples, as Americans experienced following the two rounds of stimulus checks during the pandemic. But don't count on checks in the mail.

This is at least the fourth time in this term Trump has promised Americans a payout that failed to arrive. First it was Elon Musk's "DOGE dividend," floated in February 2025 as $5,000 checks funded by savings the U.S. DOGE Service was supposed to identify; Musk left Washington a few months later having found nowhere near that amount, and the idea was quietly shelved. Then, last November, Trump promised $2,000 tariff rebate checks by the middle of 2026 — timing that would have landed the money in voters' pockets just before the midterms. That check never went out either. Now the same election-season logic is back: Trump told Ingraham the $5,000 dividend would wait until after the midterms because Democrats "can't" pass it now, even though Republicans currently control both chambers of Congress. (On Thursday, Trump separately announced that nearly a million Americans will receive $500 rebate checks before the election after being overcharged for their Obamacare plans.)

Joe Lonsdale, co-founder of Palantir and a Trump donor, tweeted from the convention: "I am hugely in favor of winning the midterms, and love much about this admin, but am strongly against bread and circus bribes."

After Trump's speech, Fox News anchor Bret Baier asked Vice President JD Vance to respond to critics describing the checks as bribery that would bankrupt the country. Vance replied that tariffs are creating "incredible wealth" and have "helped us pay down debt." That wealth might be real for lobbyists and lawyers who specialize in seeking exemptions for their clients, but they're raising prices for everyone else.

Trump's speech underscored why so many Americans who care about fiscal sanity feel politically homeless. As Trump bragged in Texas about the government taking stakes in chipmaker Intel and Venezuelan oil fields, 27-year-old state Rep. David Morales — a Bernie Sanders-endorsed democratic socialist running on rent control — beat incumbent Democratic Mayor Brett Smiley in Providence, Rhode Island's primary. A Quinnipiac poll released the same week found that 59 percent of Democrats now consider themselves supporters of the Democratic Socialists of America, versus 26 percent of voters overall.

Pandering politicians routinely promote expensive ideas in the months before elections. Usually they're offering new programs, more subsidies or tax cuts that won't be paid for. The electorate has become habituated to promises of "free" buses, "free" child care and "free" medical coverage, as well as student loan debt cancellation. Trump's checks-for-votes scheme is just more explicit.
