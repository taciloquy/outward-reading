Scientists create mice with part-human brains

科学家造出"半人脑"小鼠

Researchers have created mice with half-human brains in an effort to understand and develop new treatments for disorders such as schizophrenia, epilepsy, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability and rare forms of dementia. The scientists transplanted lab-grown human brain cells into animals that were engineered to be born without a cortex or hippocampus. This made space for the human tissue to grow inside the rodents' skulls.

研究人员创造出了拥有"半个大脑"是人类细胞的小鼠，目的是理解精神分裂症、癫痫、脑瘫、智力障碍以及罕见类型的痴呆等疾病，并开发新疗法。科学家把实验室培养出的人类脑细胞，移植到那些经过基因改造、天生没有大脑皮层或海马体的动物体内。这为人类组织在啮齿动物颅骨内生长腾出了空间。

The procedure means scientists can now take cells from patients with brain disorders, turn them into brain tissue in the laboratory, and grow that tissue in living animals.

这一流程意味着，科学家现在可以从脑疾病患者身上取得细胞，在实验室里把它们变成脑组织，再让这些组织在活的动物体内生长。

The animals can then be studied to see how the disorder takes hold in human brain tissue, and how drugs might treat the conditions.

然后就可以研究这些动物，观察疾病如何在人类脑组织中发生，以及药物可能如何治疗这些病症。

"We've been trying really hard as a community to find therapeutic solutions for these conditions, but the reality is that in psychiatry and neurology we've been left behind [by] every single branch of medicine and we have fewer therapeutics than, again, every single branch of medicine," said Sergiu Pașca, a professor of psychiatry who led the research at Stanford University. "That could be because the human brain is very complex, but it's also because the human brain is inaccessible," he added.

"作为一个群体，我们一直非常努力地想为这些疾病找到治疗方案，但现实是，在精神病学和神经病学领域，我们被医学的每一个分支都甩在了后面，可用的治疗手段也比医学的任何一个分支都少，"在斯坦福大学领导这项研究的精神病学教授塞尔久·帕什卡说。"这可能是因为人脑非常复杂，但也因为人脑难以触及，"他补充道。

"To a large extent, our goal has been to make aspects of human brain development and function accessible for investigation."

"在很大程度上，我们的目标一直是让人脑发育和功能的某些方面变得可以被研究。"

While potentially transformative for brain medicine, organoids have sparked a wave of ethical concerns, not least around whether the clumps of tissue could become conscious or feel pain, and the welfare of animals implanted with organoids. Pașca said the work had received extensive ethical oversight from the start. Experts said this would need to continue. Emily Jackson, professor of law at London School of Economics and chair of a recent report on neural organoids for the Nuffield Council on Bioethics, said: "Animal welfare is a really important concern, and it will be necessary to closely monitor these animals in order to evaluate the impact on them."

虽然可能给脑医学带来变革，类器官也引发了一连串伦理关切——尤其是这些组织团块是否会变得有意识或感到疼痛，以及被植入类器官的动物的福利。帕什卡说，这项工作从一开始就接受了广泛的伦理监督。专家表示，这种监督需要持续下去。伦敦政治经济学院法学教授、纽菲尔德生物伦理学理事会近期一份神经类器官报告的主席埃米莉·杰克逊说："动物福利是一个非常重要的关切，有必要密切监测这些动物，以评估对它们的影响。"

Writing in Nature, Pașca describes how the mice were injected with human brain organoids that were themselves created by reprogramming donated skin cells. The newborn mice received several injections, each containing about 100,000 human brain cells, into the space where their own brain tissue was missing. In total, the rodents lacked about 14m mouse brain cells and ended up with about 4m human ones – half the brain by volume.

在《自然》杂志上撰文时，帕什卡描述了实验过程：小鼠被注射了人类脑类器官，而这些类器官本身是通过重编程捐赠的皮肤细胞培育出来的。新生小鼠接受了数次注射，每次约含 10 万个人类脑细胞，注入它们自身脑组织缺失的空腔。这些啮齿动物总共缺少约 1400 万个自身脑细胞，最终拥有约 400 万个人类脑细胞——按体积算约占大脑的一半。

Three months after surgery, the human tissue had hooked up to the mouse's blood supply and almost entirely filled the cavity, taking up about half the size of the rodent's brain. Some of the human neurons formed connections with mouse brain cells and spinal cord. The human neurons were not structured or wired up in the same way as in people, and the brain tissue was immature, equivalent to that found halfway through human pregnancy. Tests on the "xenocortical" mice showed the animals were not enhanced by the transplants, but their shaky gait and cognitive problems did improve a little.

手术三个月后，人类组织已经接入了小鼠的血液供应，并几乎填满整个空腔，占到啮齿动物大脑体积的约一半。部分人类神经元与小鼠脑细胞和脊髓建立了连接。这些人类神经元的结构和连接方式与人体内的并不相同，脑组织也不成熟，相当于人类妊娠中期水平。对这些"异种皮层"小鼠的测试显示，移植并没有让它们变强，但它们摇晃的步态和认知问题确有轻微改善。

To demonstrate how the mice could shed light on human brain disorders, the researchers exposed some of the animals to five hours of low oxygen. This showed how vulnerable human nerve cells are to oxygen deprivation, which in pregnancy and birth can cause cerebral palsy. The researchers found that human brain tissue in the mice contained rare cells known as von Economo neurons, which have only been seen in postmortem examinations. The cells are among the first to die in frontotemporal dementia, a rare form of the disease, which Pașca now hopes to study in xenocortical mice.

为展示这些小鼠如何帮助揭示人类脑疾病，研究人员让部分动物处于低氧环境五小时。这显示出人类神经细胞对缺氧有多脆弱——在怀孕和分娩过程中，缺氧可能导致脑瘫。研究人员还发现，小鼠体内的人类脑组织含有一种名为冯·埃科诺莫神经元（von Economo neurons）的罕见细胞，此前只在尸检中见过。这类细胞是额颞叶痴呆（一种罕见痴呆）中最先死亡的细胞之一，帕什卡现在希望在异种皮层小鼠中研究这种疾病。

Prof Madeline Lancaster, group leader at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge, said the approach was best suited to questions about disorders and treatments where a whole animal was needed. "It's less clear to me how this will inform our understanding of human brain development since it is rather artificial, and not at all like how the brain develops naturally," she said.

剑桥医学研究委员会分子生物学实验室的课题组负责人玛德琳·兰开斯特教授说，这种方法最适合回答那些需要完整动物的疾病与治疗问题。"至于它如何增进我们对人脑发育的理解，我不太清楚，因为它相当人为，完全不像大脑自然发育的方式，"她说。

Many researchers working on brain organoids are growing them in vitro, or in dishes. The hope is they help to reduce the number of animals used in research.

许多研究脑类器官的人是在体外、也就是培养皿中培养它们的，希望这有助于减少研究中使用的动物数量。

"It's obviously ethically sensitive and there needs to be a very good reason to do this type of animal experimentation, which most scientific avenues do not require," Lancaster added. "The field is still aiming for fully in vitro solutions, but this work can also inform on what may be needed to improve those in vitro models to reach more mature stages."

"这显然在伦理上很敏感，做这类动物实验需要有非常充分的理由，而大多数科研方向并不需要，"兰开斯特补充说，"这个领域仍在追求完全体外的方案，但这项工作也能提示：要让那些体外模型达到更成熟的阶段，可能需要什么。"
