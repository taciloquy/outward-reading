Is this the end for China's love of the English language?

中国人学英语的热情要终结了吗？

Half a century ago, learning English went almost overnight from being discouraged to a national obsession. Throughout the 1980s and beyond, young and old alike would gather in parks, at what became known as "English corners", to practise with each other. The language became a core part of the national curriculum, on a par with maths and Chinese, right up to the gaokao, or university entrance exam.

半个世纪前，学英语几乎在一夜之间从"不被鼓励"变成了全民狂热。整个 1980 年代及以后，老老少少会聚在公园里彼此练习，这种地方后来被称为"英语角"。英语成了国家课程的核心部分，与数学、语文并列，一直贯穿到高考。

Now a prominent teacher and education blogger has reignited a national debate by questioning whether learning English is necessary at all. "Our children desperately begin learning English even before they have properly mastered their native language," Tang Jiafeng wrote. "Yet we are a civilisation that dates back thousands of years. English is just a tool. It's enough to be able to use it. "

如今，一位知名教师兼教育博主语出惊人，重新点燃了全国性争论：学英语到底还有没有必要。"我们的孩子在本族语还没真正掌握之前，就拼命开始学英语了，"唐家峰写道，"可我们是一个延续数千年的文明。英语只是个工具，会用就够了。"

The debate touches on questions of whether children are over-pressured by schools and parents and the suitability of traditional rote-learning techniques. It has also acquired a new dimension as artificial-intelligence translation tools improve. With technology increasingly capable of translating languages almost instantly, some young people are asking whether years of intensive English study will remain as valuable as it once was. At the same time, uncertainty about graduate employment has prompted a wider discussion about which skills students will actually need when they enter the workforce.

这场争论牵扯出一系列问题：孩子是否被学校和家长施加了过重压力，以及传统的死记硬背方法是否合适。随着人工智能翻译工具的进步，它也有了新的维度。当技术越来越能做到近乎即时的语言互译，一些年轻人开始问：多年高强度的英语学习是否还像过去那样有价值。与此同时，对大学毕业生就业的不确定性，也引发了关于学生进入职场究竟需要哪些技能的更广泛讨论。

Tang, who has a PhD in mathematics and offers tuition for postgraduate entrance exams, has established himself as a popular voice in the competitive world of online advice for students. His most recent post was a reaction to announcements by provincial education authorities that they were downgrading noncore subjects like biology, geography and history for high-school entrance exams to reduce the pressure on teenagers. Liaoning province, for example, recently removed history, biology and geography from its middle-school entry test as part of efforts to reduce students' workload.

拥有数学博士学位、并提供考研辅导的唐家峰，已在竞争激烈的学生网络建议领域确立了自己热门声音的地位。他最近那篇帖子，是对几个省级教育部门宣布降低生物、地理、历史等非核心科目在中考中的比重（以减轻青少年压力）的回应。例如辽宁省最近就把历史、生物和地理从中学入学考试中取消，作为给学生减负的一部分。

Tang claimed that this was effectively prioritising a subject, English, that would be "completely irrelevant to the majority of people in their future professional lives". English remains a compulsory subject in China's basic education system alongside Chinese and mathematics. Under the traditional 750-point gaokao structure, each of those three subjects is worth 150 points, even as the weighting and assessment of other subjects have undergone reforms.

唐家峰称，这实际上是在优先重视一门"对大多数人未来职业生涯完全无关"的科目——英语。在中国的基础教育体系中，英语仍是必修科目，与语文、数学并列。在传统的 750 分高考结构下，这三门每门都是 150 分，尽管其他科目的权重和评价方式已经历改革。

The most prominent immediate response came from Hu Xijin, a former editor of the English-language tabloid Global Times. He accused Tang of being "biased and narrow-minded" and urged education departments to ignore him. One argument favoured by critics of English is that when native English speakers are so poor at learning Chinese — a fairly indisputable claim — why should Chinese people learn their language?

最引人注目的即时回应来自胡锡进——英文小报《环球时报》的前总编辑。他指责唐家峰"有偏见、心胸狭隘"，并呼吁教育部门别理他。批评英语的人爱用的一个论点是：既然英语母语者学中文那么差——这一点基本无可争辩——中国人为什么要学他们的语言？

That ignored the fact that, like it or not, English was the world's lingua franca, Hu said. "More than 90 per cent of international scientific academic papers are produced in English," he wrote. "If a person wants to advance to a higher level, being familiar with and mastering English is one of the most important educational preparations. " The debate has also raised questions about access to international knowledge and opportunities, particularly for students who may later work or study across borders.

胡锡进说，这忽略了一个事实：不管你喜不喜欢，英语都是世界的通用语言。"超过 90% 的国际科学学术论文是用英语写成的，"他写道，"一个人想要往上走，熟悉并掌握英语是最重要的教育准备之一。"这场争论还引发了关于获取国际知识与机会的质疑，尤其是对于那些以后可能跨境工作或求学的学生而言。

Another argument against English is that it is a sign of elitism; in practice, only the upper middle classes can afford to send their children to universities in the West, or travel there. Hu argued that downgrading English would make elitism worse, since the upper classes would find new ways to pay for their children to learn English anyway. Critics of weakening English in public education have similarly argued that wealthier families could simply buy private tuition, while students from less privileged backgrounds would have fewer opportunities to acquire the same skills.

另一个反对英语的理由是：它是精英主义的象征——现实中，只有中上阶层才负担得起送孩子去西方读大学或出国旅行。胡锡进反驳说，弱化英语只会让精英主义更严重，因为上层阶级总会找到新的门路花钱让孩子学英语。同样，批评在公立教育中弱化英语的人认为，富裕家庭大可以直接买私教，而来自弱势背景的学生获得同样技能的机会会更少。

Another intervention, admittedly from a professor with a vested interest, was more blunt. "Let me ask: is maths useful in everyday life? " wrote Cao Wen, who teaches at Beijing Foreign Studies University, a breeding ground for diplomats. "Ordinary people go to work, buy groceries, watch movies. Do we really need to use functions or calculate square roots every day? Yet we still expect children to learn maths. If you judge whether a subject is necessary simply by whether people will use it in their daily lives, can you really call yourself an educator? "

另一位发声者——坦白说，是一位有切身利益关系的教授——说得更直白。"我问一句：数学在日常生活中有用吗？"北京外国语大学（外交官的摇篮）教师曹文写道，"普通人上班、买菜、看电影。我们真的每天都需要用函数或算平方根吗？可我们仍然要求孩子学数学。如果仅仅以'人们日常生活中会不会用到'来判断一门课有没有必要，你真的配称自己为教育者吗？"
