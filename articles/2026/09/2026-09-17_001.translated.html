Boomers Are Sacrificing Retirement Savings to Support Their Grandkids

婴儿潮一代正牺牲退休储蓄补贴孙辈

The Bank of Mom and Dad is staying open for the grandkids as the cost of raising a child in the US tops $300,000 for the first time this year — and that doesn't include college tuition. Nearly two in five parents of young children expect to receive financial help — or ask for it — from their own parents or grandparents in the next year. Among them,47% said they will receive cash for day-to-day basics from relatives, according to the BMO Real Financial Progress Index survey. A quarter said they expect relatives to contribute to their children's 529 or other savings accounts.

随着今年在美国养育一个孩子的成本首次突破 30 万美元（这还不含大学学费），"爸妈银行"也为孙辈继续开门营业。近五分之二的婴幼儿父母表示，未来一年指望从自己的父母或（孩子的）祖父母那里拿到——或开口索要——经济帮助。根据 BMO 真实财务进展指数调查，其中 47% 说会从亲戚那里拿到用于日常开销的现金。四分之一说指望亲戚为孩子的 529 或其他储蓄账户出钱。

"Families trying to figure out how they're going to solve for these increased costs are really trying to lean on their extended families," said Robin Growley, BMO's US head of consumer products. Parents with family support said they saved $1,915 a year on childcare and babysitting, and $1,443 on groceries.

"那些正琢磨怎么消化这些上涨成本的家庭，确实在使劲依靠他们的大家庭，"BMO 美国消费产品业务负责人罗宾·格劳利说。有家庭支持的父母表示，他们在育儿和保姆上每年省下 1915 美元，在食品杂货上省下 1443 美元。

Gisele Ayora, a 36-year-old marketing professional and mother of three, said the biggest financial boosts she got from her in-laws were housing and childcare. They bought her family a $600,000, six-bedroom home in Manahawkin, New Jersey. They asked the couple to pay about $1,700 a month — roughly what they had been paying to rent a two-bedroom apartment — about half of what a mortgage would've cost them. Her mother-in-law also stepped in to watch the kids when Ayora had to work late or travel for her job.

36 岁的市场营销从业者、三个孩子的母亲吉塞勒·阿约拉说，公婆给她的最大经济支持是住房和育儿。他们给她家买了一套位于新泽西州马纳霍金的 60 万美元六居室住宅。他们让这对夫妻每月付大约 1700 美元——大致等于他们此前租两居室的租金，约为房贷成本的一半。当阿约拉因工作加班或出差时，婆婆也会出面照看孩子。

For some young families, the help can feel like getting part of an inheritance early, when they need it most, said Lindsey Stanberry, founder of the personal finance website The Purse. Baby Boomers are sitting on a lot of wealth, while their adult children are facing steep housing and childcare costs. But for grandparents without as much to spare, that support can eat into their own retirement plans.

个人理财网站 The Purse 创始人林赛·斯坦伯里说，对一些年轻家庭来说，这种帮助感觉像是提前拿到了一部分遗产，而且是在他们最需要的时候。婴儿潮一代坐拥大量财富，而他们的成年子女正面临高昂的住房和育儿成本。但对那些余钱不多的祖父母来说，这种支持会侵蚀他们自己的退休计划。

Haron Marlee estimates she spent $24,000 in one year covering everything from diapers to gas to car insurance for her 36-year-old daughter, Melissa, and grandchildren. She went from a semi-retired therapist to a regular babysitter and even picked up bartending shifts to help support them. "Those burdens fundamentally changed my finances, my retirement plans," said Marlee, a 59-year-old who now lives in Ocean County, New Jersey. Her daughter, a law school graduate, said she was initially embarrassed by the situation. But when her marriage ended, she struggled to find a job and it was the best option to stay afloat.

哈伦·马利估计，她一年为 36 岁的女儿梅丽莎和孙辈花了 2.4 万美元，涵盖从尿布、汽油到汽车保险的一切。她从半退休的心理治疗师变成全职保姆，甚至还去酒吧兼职帮忙贴补她们。"这些负担从根本上改变了我的财务状况、我的退休计划，"现年 59 岁、住在新泽西州海洋县的马利说。她的女儿是法学院毕业生，说一开始对这种处境感到难堪。但当她的婚姻结束、又很难找到工作时，这是能维持下去的最好选择。

Financial advisers warn parents against sacrificing their own retirement savings to keep adult children afloat. Lorie Jones, owner of Fearless Financial Advisors in Colorado, said 20% to 25% of her clients are covering a significant share of their adult children's expenses well into their late 20s and early 30s. "I do have to talk to some of those parents and say, 'You need to take care of you first'," she said. "'You've got to prioritize your own retirement'."

理财顾问警告父母不要为了让成年子女周转而牺牲自己的退休储蓄。科罗拉多州 Fearless Financial Advisors 的老板洛里·琼斯说，她 20% 到 25% 的客户在子女快三十岁、三十出头时仍在承担他们很大一部分开支。"我确实得跟其中一些父母说：'你得先照顾好你自己'，"她说，"'你必须把自己的退休放在优先位置'。"

Barb Green knows that strain firsthand. Her 29-year-old son moved back home in 2017 after a semester of college and has stayed there rent free since. Green said the job market and broader economy have made it difficult for him to move out. He doesn't yet have kids, but supporting him has put a dent in Green and her husband's retirement plans. "The money that we've been planning on putting away for retirement to travel and do fun things during retirement — since we've spent all of our life working — I feel like we are not able to plan that or do that because it's like we're still raising him because he's still living there."

巴布·格林对这种压力有切身体会。她 29 岁的儿子 2017 年上了一学期大学后就搬回家住，此后一直免租住着。格林说，就业市场和整体经济让他难以搬出去。他还没有孩子，但供养他已经在格林和丈夫的退休计划上砸出一个坑。"我们一直打算把钱存起来，退休后去旅行、做些有意思的事——毕竟我们辛苦工作了一辈子——可我觉得我们没法再规划或实现这些了，因为他还住在家里，感觉就像我们还在抚养他。"

She's also seen the dynamic through her work. Green is a coach at Community Connections for Children in Pennsylvania, an organization that helps families find and apply for childcare. She said that she's seen a roughly 60% increase in grandparents caring for their grandchildren in the past five years.

她也在工作中看到这种变化。格林是宾夕法尼亚州"儿童社区联结"机构的一名教练，该组织帮助家庭寻找和申请育儿服务。她说，过去五年里，照看孙辈的祖父母增加了大约 60%。

For Marlee, the semi-retired therapist, the strain was more than financial. "It took a toll on me that I had not anticipated," she said. She's since started a Facebook group and written an advice book for parents of adult children called Stop Raising Adult Children: Break the Cycle of Blame and Take Your Life Back. Marlee tells parents financially supporting their adult children to set ground rules and boundaries — and, importantly, an end date for their contribution. She also tells them to protect their retirement.

对半退休治疗师马利来说，这种压力不只是钱的问题。"它对我的损耗是我没预料到的，"她说。她后来建了一个 Facebook 群，并写了一本给成年子女父母的劝告书《停止抚养成年子女：打破指责的循环，把生活拿回来》。马利告诉那些在经济上支持成年子女的父母：要立下基本规则和边界——更重要的是，给自己的付出设一个截止日期。她还告诉他们要保住自己的退休金。

Eventually, she and her daughter made a more formal arrangement. Melissa would help out through "sweat equity" by cooking meals and cleaning. Once she secured work, she could live with her for free for one more year. Since then, she's had to start contributing to household expenses and rent. "You cannot jump into the riptide with your children," Marlee said. "You can only lend them a hand."

最终，她和女儿达成了更正式的安排：梅丽莎以"劳力入股"的方式帮忙做饭、打扫。等她找到工作后，可以再免费住一年。此后她必须开始分担家用和房租。"你不能跟着孩子一起跳进激流，"马利说，"你只能伸一只手拉他们一把。"
