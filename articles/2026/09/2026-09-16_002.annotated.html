The Most Effective Way to Learn Is to Push Through It the Hard Way

真正高效的学习，就是咬牙硬扛过难关
There is a comforting myth about learning—that if you just find 
                      想当然的说法（≠神话）
the right method, the right teacher, or the right shortcut, 
everything will suddenly become easy.
People spend years searching for the perfect system, hoping it 
will eliminate struggle altogether.
But the truth is far less appealing: real learning is not 
designed to feel easy.
At its core, learning is a process of friction. It requires you 
                                      /ˈfrɪkʃn/摩擦，阻力
to sit with confusion, to face what you don’t understand, and to 
  忍受，与（困惑）共处
stay in that discomfort long enough for clarity to emerge.
             /dɪsˈkʌmfət/不适/ˈklærəti/清晰，豁然开朗
This is what people often call hard learning—not because it is 
inefficient, but because it demands effort without immediate 
/ˌɪnɪˈfɪʃnt/低效的
reward.
When people avoid this stage, they turn to passive forms of 
                                  转而求助于/ˈpæsɪv/被动的
learning.
They watch videos, read summaries, highlight sentences, and feel 
productive.
But passive exposure creates familiarity, not mastery. It feels 
    /ˈpæsɪv/被动的/ɪkˈspəʊʒə(r)/接触，浏览/ˈmɑːstəri/精通
like progress, but it rarely leads to real understanding.
Hard learning looks very different.
It is slower, quieter, and often frustrating.
It involves rereading the same paragraph multiple times, 
attempting problems without knowing the answer, and failing 
repeatedly before getting it right.
There is no instant gratification—only gradual improvement.
                    /ˌɡrætɪfɪˈkeɪʃn/即时满足
One of the most important elements of hard learning is repetition
.
Not mechanical repetition, but deliberate repetition—returning to
    /məˈkænɪkl/机械的/dɪˈlɪbərət/刻意的
the same material with deeper attention each time.
With every pass, your understanding shifts. What was once 
confusing becomes familiar; what was once familiar becomes clear;
what was once clear becomes flexible.
Another key aspect is active engagement.
                             /ɪnˈɡeɪdʒmənt/主动投入
Hard learning requires you to think, not just consume.
It asks you to explain ideas in your own words, test your 
understanding, and apply knowledge in new contexts.
This is where knowledge transforms into ability.
The difficulty of hard learning often discourages people. It 
feels inefficient compared to faster, easier alternatives.
      /ˌɪnɪˈfɪʃnt/低效的
But what seems efficient in the short term is often ineffective 
in the long term.
Learning that avoids struggle rarely survives beyond the moment.
There is also a psychological barrier. Many people equate 
                              /ˈbæriə(r)/障碍/ɪˈkweɪt/等同看待
difficulty with failure.
When something feels hard, they assume they are not good at it.
In reality, difficulty is not a sign of inadequacy—it is a sign 
                                        /ɪnˈædɪkwəsi/不足，无能感
that learning is happening.
If you look at people who achieve deep expertise in any field, 
                                       /ˌekspɜːˈtiːz/专长
their process is rarely glamorous.
                        /ˈɡlæmərəs/光鲜的
It is built on long hours of focused effort, repeated attempts, 
and a willingness to stay with complexity.
They don’t avoid hard learning—they rely on it.
So when people say, The best way to learn is to push through, it 
                                               咬牙挺过去
is not a rejection of good methods or smart strategies.
It is a recognition that no method can replace effort. Techniques
can guide you, but they cannot do the work for you.
In the end, learning is not about avoiding difficulty—it is about
building the capacity to move through it.
             /kəˈpæsəti/能力，容量
And the sooner you accept that, the sooner your learning becomes 
real.
