Anthropic Targets $1.4 Trillion Valuation in Blockbuster IPO
Anthropic 剑指 1.4 万亿美元估值，上演一场爆炸级的上市
Paragraph 1
Anthropic PBC is seeking a fully diluted valuation of about $1.4 trillion in its initial public offering, in a bid to make history as the first frontier artificial intelligence lab to go public. The Claude chatbot maker and some of its backers are seeking to raise as much as $90.4 billion in the IPO and a private placement. The base offering would raise up to $64.4 billion, with shares marketed for $70 to $78 each. At the top of the price range, Anthropic would have a market value of about $1 trillion, based on the shares outstanding listed in the filing. Accounting for employee stock options, restricted shares and stock reserved for the company’s charitable donation matching program, Anthropic would have a fully diluted value of about $1.4 trillion.
Paragraph 2
Anthropic’s ambitious target, including the private placement, would hit roughly $100 billion when including a so-called over-allotment option or greenshoe. Investors are under pressure to decide whether valuations in AI reflect a plausible trajectory for a technology that many expect to transform the economy and society as a whole, or overconfidence in an investment theme that hasn’t yet proven its staying power.
Paragraph 3
The widespread adoption of Claude Code and rival tools by software developers points to the potential transformation of a wide range of professional activity, from discovering new miracle drugs to navigating complex legal and accounting rules to running a factory. Already, more than 90% of contributions to Anthropic’s code base were written by Claude.
Paragraph 4
Sales are surging. The company reported third-quarter revenue of nearly $17 billion as of Sept. 30, versus $1.4 billion in the same period a year ago. Keeping the pace of development running at a breakneck pace will require growing revenue faster than Anthropic’s spending on AI infrastructure. Anthropic has $895 billion of commitments, most of which are for computing power, chips, the filing shows. About $70 billion worth — a figure that’s larger than the individual market caps of roughly two-thirds of S&P 500 companies — are due just in the year through September 2027.
Paragraph 5
Unlike other tech companies with sources of income such as advertising to fall back on, Anthropic will have to convince investors that its focus on AI alone will generate profit far beyond the technology’s surging costs. SpaceX may have tied its aspirations to AI when it pitched investors on its IPO, but most of its actual revenue at the time came from its other business lines.
Paragraph 6
The speed of development has become increasingly controversial, with Anthropic’s own Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei among those calling for top AI labs to “pace the frontier” in order to prioritize safety. Anthropic itself warned that the technology could pose “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity” in its IPO filing, if the technology isn’t developed with adequate safeguards and oversight. Regulators, investors and even Anthropic’s employees have flagged the significant risk of humans losing control of the technology, with one putting the chance of AI eliminating all humans in the next decade at greater than 10%. Bill Gates said AI is powerful enough to drive events that cause “a billion deaths.”
Paragraph 7
Anthropic’s rival OpenAI is targeting a 2027 listing and Chief Executive Officer Sam Altman called 2026 an “ill-advised moment” for an IPO, citing concern over AI safety. The company is raising funds at a $1.25 trillion valuation, people familiar with the matter have said.
Paragraph 8
Anthropic’s origin story centers around former OpenAI executives defecting because they wanted to prioritize AI safety. Nearly six years after its founding, Anthropic has structured itself as a public benefit corporation that balances shareholder interests with those of society, and whose governance includes a Long-Term Benefit Trust that has the power to elect and remove a majority of the members of the company’s board.
Paragraph 9
The company’s total operating expenses jumped to $17.5 billion in the third quarter, a 292% increase from a year prior. It flipped from an operating profit of $974 million in the second quarter — its first after at least five quarters of operating losses — back to a negative $567 million in the three months ended Sept. 30. The operating loss in the third quarter was primarily driven by $5 billion of computing costs under short-term commitments and $2 billion of non-cash charges related to the company’s charitable donation-matching program.
Paragraph 10
AI pricing pressures have begun to hit Anthropic’s revenue. Lower prices on some of its new models, plus contractual discounts for some customers, reduced its revenue growth in the third quarter by about $600 million. Anthropic has cut prices in newer model rollouts, including Opus 5.5 in September, and OpenAI has also introduced lower-priced models in recent months. The AI giants face growing competition from cheaper open-weight models, including from Chinese developers, which customers can download and fine-tune to their needs.
