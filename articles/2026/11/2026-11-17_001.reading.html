一场让黄仁勋重回英伟达CEO之位的运动

The Wall Street Journal · 标题
The Campaign to Bring Back Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO
一场让黄仁勋重回英伟达CEO之位的运动
Paragraph 1
Amjad Masad was recording a promotional video for his AI company on Friday afternoon when he got an urgent text: Could he help the campaign to get Jensen Huang his job back? An Nvidia employee had used Masad’s service, Replit, to build a website where anyone could sign an open letter demanding that Nvidia’s board, which ousted Huang as chief executive last week, reinstate him. The “Stand With Jensen” website was struggling under the load of traffic it was receiving. Masad inspected the website’s code and found that the petition was limited to 200 signatures a minute—far below the rate of submissions it was seeing. He lifted the cap and added a Captcha to limit bots, among other fixes.
Paragraph 2
As of Sunday afternoon, the petition had 106,000 names, including confirmed signatures from Masad, Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, tech investor Gavin Baker, Shopify CEO Tobias Lutke and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney. Some people, including prominent Nvidia employees, said their names appeared but that they hadn’t signed the petition.
Paragraph 3
Across Silicon Valley, a constellation of Huang’s peers, employees and investors are holding on to hope he could regain his position atop the chip maker—even as official scrutiny of his role in providing powerful artificial-intelligence chips to China intensifies. On Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged Nvidia’s board to bring in an outside candidate as CEO: “Just what was going on inside the company, they need a dramatic shake-up,” he said on Fox News.
Paragraph 4
In the days since Huang stepped down following reports of affairs with two women, well-known tech CEOs and investors have registered their continued support for him—in public social-media posts and in private phone calls. In a Friday open letter, Baker and Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner said Huang “has earned the right for a deliberate process that avoids any needless rush to replace him before all the facts are known.” On Friday Nvidia’s board released a statement saying it was hiring an executive search firm and hadn’t yet engaged any potential candidates.
Paragraph 5
U.S. officials suspect Alice Huang of passing information to Chinese intelligence during the relationship. Her company, Megaspeed, became a cloud partner of Nvidia, allowing it to purchase large numbers of chips. Jensen Huang’s supporters argue that the former CEO is pro-American and essential to the movement to develop American-controlled open-source AI, ensuring the technology isn’t ultimately controlled by a small number of companies. They say that policies espoused by China hawks would backfire, enabling Chinese chip giant Huawei to make up ground on Nvidia.
Paragraph 6
Masad said that the vibe in his group chat of tech industry peers was of concern that a champion of open source was being taken down. Open-source software permits users to download and alter the code for themselves. “He’s been very helpful to companies and he’s a good counterbalance to voices saying we need to regulate AI and shut down open models,” said Masad. Nvidia employees who confirmed signing the open letter said they view Huang as an irreplaceable leader. Some employees have discussed holding a demonstration in Huang’s support on the company’s Santa Clara, Calif., campus in coming days.
Paragraph 7
Bessent in the Fox News interview called for deeper inquiries into Nvidia’s practice of selling huge quantities of chips to companies in Southeast Asia that serve Chinese AI companies and that in some cases are suspected of diverting chips into China. Bessent said the appearance of a “culture of noncompliance at Nvidia” had persuaded him “that as the board conducts a search for a new leader, that they need to go outside the company.” Colette Kress, who was named interim CEO after Huang’s resignation, served under him as chief financial officer for 13 years. “I want to keep coming back to what was the culture at Nvidia, and were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?” Bessent said. “And I think we need answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel.”
Paragraph 8
Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, a professor and governance expert at Yale University’s School of Management, said the grounds for Huang’s dismissal were solid under the company’s own code of conduct. “If they were to look the other way, then it’s open season, tear up the rulebook, because nothing is legally enforceable in the company’s own ethical standards,” Sonnenfeld said. Huang’s involvement in deals with Megaspeed and other companies accused of helping China get around chip-export controls will also continue to draw scrutiny. “This was aiding and abetting the circumvention of U.S. trade laws,” he said.
