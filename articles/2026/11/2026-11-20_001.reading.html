mogging拿下2026年度词汇：9个新词，看懂这一年人们在焦虑什么

Quartz · 标题
9 words from Collins' 2026 shortlist, from mogging to Manchesterism
Paragraph 1
Each year, people need names for what they are living through and arguing about, and the new words they settle on show what those things were. In 2026, those needs came from almost every corner of life: slang, science, sport, politics, shopping for flavors and looking for work. Campaigners promoted others on purpose, researchers coined some for their own work, and a sports body wrote one into a tournament’s rules. A year’s new vocabulary is therefore a snapshot of competing forces rather than of a single trend. The more telling question is less which word took the title than how each one got there.
Paragraph 2
Collins looks for its candidates in what people write, and much of that writing now happens online. As a result, words that people pass quickly around online circles have an advantage. Several of this year’s candidates, though, did not reach the dictionary by going viral. Someone with a purpose put them into circulation.
Paragraph 3
Mogging is not a new word, and Collins chose it for how sharply its use climbed in 2026. Collins recorded a 210% rise in its use this year, LBC reports, and named it the Collins Word of the Year 2026. The jump in use came from Gen Z and Gen Alpha, who took up the word ironically. In their hands, to mog something simply means to outdo it. The word grew out of the slang mog, short for AMOG. Slang can be ironic and still say something sincere about what a generation measures itself against.
Paragraph 4
People often want something long before they have a short name for it, and “bixie” names one of those requests. Collins shortlisted bixie for a hairstyle that falls between a bob and a pixie in length, according to LBC. It joins the start of “bob” to the end of “pixie,” so anyone who knows both styles can work out what a bixie is the first time they hear the word. People take up some words simply because they make an ordinary request easier to say.
Paragraph 5
Carspreading entered the language through a campaign rather than through slang. The word belongs to the campaign that Clean Cities launched in February 2025, according to road.cc. The campaigners built their complaint into the name. It treats bigger cars as a problem of space, so anyone who uses the word also repeats the argument. Lord wants far more councils to act in 2027, because only two in the U.K. have taken action so far, according to road.cc. The campaigners are still waiting for councils to act on it.
Paragraph 6
Firewave began as a researchers’ term rather than as a joke, a meme or a slogan. Collins shortlisted the word, which it defines as the spread of wildfires, for its 2026 Word of the Year, according to LBC. Researchers at Imperial College London coined the word only in 2025, Wildfire Today reports, so a scientific coinage was in the running for a general dictionary’s title almost as soon as it existed. The warning behind it concerns cities, not only the remote forest blazes most readers picture when they hear “wildfire.” Ordinary readers can understand firewave without any science, because its shape copies a word they already hear every summer. The researchers made the term to help fire services prepare before a dangerous day, not to describe one afterward.
Paragraph 7
Friendslop went from a single social media post to a dictionary shortlist faster than most slang does. The word spread while Peak, a game built to be played with friends, sold more than 10 million copies after its release in June 2025, according to CBC News. Players enjoyed these games before anyone named them, and the name they got sounds more like a jab than praise.
Paragraph 8
“Ghost job” came out of the hunt for work, where applicants try to work out whether an advertised role is real. Collins shortlisted the term for 2026 to describe a job that an employer advertises with no intention to appoint anyone, according to LBC. That doubt is common: 58% of job seekers in a ResumeBuilder.com poll said that an interview they attended had seemed, looking back, to be for a role nobody meant to fill, according to NBC 26. Some companies keep a listing up after they have already chosen a candidate. Others use it to gauge the pool of talent or to collect resumes. Job seekers therefore use the word as much to complain as to describe.
Paragraph 9
Hydration break names a pause that FIFA wrote into the rules of the 2026 World Cup. FIFA made the pause a routine part of the 2026 World Cup and required it even in covered stadiums and cool weather, according to ABC News. Players’ health and television coverage both had a stake in the rule.
Paragraph 10
Manchesterism rose with a politician rather than through everyday talk. The word is among the candidates for Collins’ standout new term of 2026, according to LBC, and it names a way of running a country. Manchesterism is a claim about where power should sit. Its central idea is handing government down to communities, according to The Associated Press. Manchesterism may name a program that does not exist yet, and Burnham will settle its meaning in office.
